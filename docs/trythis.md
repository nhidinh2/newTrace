hi Given that you’re trying to follow AI research + theoretical/applied math + quant/finance, I would not try to follow dozens of sites. A small high-quality stack is much better. •	AI — daily/weekly awareness ◦	The Batch (DeepLearning.AI) — probably the best single weekly overview. It covers models, research, industry, hardware, policy, and applications without requiring you to read everything yourself. It is still actively publishing in 2026. ◦	Interconnects — Nathan Lambert — much more technical. Excellent for frontier/open models, post-training, RL, scaling, and understanding why new models matter rather than just hearing that they launched. ◦	Import AI — Jack Clark — good intersection of AI research, safety, policy, and societal implications. ◦	OpenAI Research + Anthropic News + Google DeepMind blog — read the primary source when something important actually launches. All three are actively publishing research/model updates. ◦	For actual papers, watch arXiv cs.LG, cs.AI, stat.ML, cs.DS, plus OpenReview around ICLR/NeurIPS/ICML. ◦	Since you're interested in AI safety/evals, add METR and Epoch AI rather than relying only on general AI news. •	Math — research and discoveries ◦	Quanta Mathematics would be my #1. It manages to cover genuine research breakthroughs while explaining why they matter. Right now its math feed is covering graph theory, Langlands, combinatorics, AI + mathematics and other active areas. ◦	Quanta also now has Transformation, a weekly dispatch specifically about math + AI, which is unusually well aligned with your interests. ◦	Follow arXiv more narrowly rather than /math/recent: math.CO for combinatorics, math.PR for probability, math.NT for number theory, and whichever analysis/geometry categories your research moves into. ◦	Terence Tao's blog is worth following. His posts are often closer to how mathematicians actually think about developments, and he recently started collecting resources specifically around AI and mathematics. ◦	AMS Math in the Media is a slower secondary source for broader mathematical developments. ◦	MathOverflow isn't really news, but browsing its hot questions occasionally is a good way to see what working mathematicians are discussing. •	Finance / markets ◦	Reuters Markets — use this as your factual, fast daily news source. I would read Reuters before Twitter/X takes about why the market moved. ◦	Financial Times — better for understanding the macro/business story behind the move. FT Alphaville in particular is excellent if you're interested in market structure, strange trades, financial data and quant-ish topics; it's actively publishing in 2026. ◦	Bloomberg — excellent if you have access. Markets Daily for markets and Matt Levine's Money Stuff for learning how securities, deals, derivatives and Wall Street actually work. Bloomberg currently lists both as active newsletters. ◦	FRED — don't just read people talking about economic data. Look at CPI, unemployment, yields, credit spreads, etc. yourself. FRED updates the underlying economic series as releases come out. ◦	Federal Reserve releases/FOMC minutes — primary source whenever monetary policy actually matters instead of trying to interpret screenshots of Powell quotes. ◦	For your quant interests, I'd add FT Alphaville + academic papers/SSRN/arXiv q-fin rather than spending much time on ordinary stock-picking news.
